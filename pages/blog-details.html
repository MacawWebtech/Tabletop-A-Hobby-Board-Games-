Wingspan, Two Years On: Does It Still Hold Up?

Meera Rao September 2, 2026 7 min read

A shop-floor review, not a first impression

Most Wingspan reviews are written after a handful of plays, fresh off the wonder of unboxing 170 unique bird cards. We've had a different vantage point: two demo copies on our shelves have been played more than 200 times between them, mostly by first-timers we're teaching on the spot.

That repetition changes what you notice. The things that make Wingspan a brilliant first hobby game are still there — but so are a few seams that only show after the fiftieth teach.

Where it still shines

The birdfeeder dice tower remains one of the best pieces of physical design in modern board games — it does real mechanical work while being genuinely satisfying to shake. And the low-conflict engine-building still lands for players who find direct-confrontation games stressful.

New players consistently tell us the same thing: they understand their turn, even in round four, because each bird's power reads in plain language on the card.

Where the shine has worn

At higher player counts, downtime between turns is real — watching four other engines run before your next action can drag a first game to over an hour. And the "bird facts" flavour text, charming at first, gets skipped entirely by regulars.

Who should still buy it in 2026

If you're building a first hobby-game shelf, or want something that welcomes non-gamers without talking down to them, Wingspan remains an easy recommendation. If you already own two or three engine-builders, it's less essential — but still one of the best-looking games you'll ever put on a table.
